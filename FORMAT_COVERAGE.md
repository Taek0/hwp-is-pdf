## Korean slant, scripts and baseline offsets (2026-10-14)

- HWP bits 15/16 and HWPX supscript/subscript feed the same glyph metrics and rendering.
- Native italic fonts stay native; glyphs without an italic face (including Hangul)
  use a per-glyph 12-degree slant around the shifted baseline. PDF and QA share the
  matrix; the transform ends immediately after the glyph and composes with groups.
- Scripts use 65% nominal size, with a -35% superscript/+15% subscript baseline
  shift. These are explicit approximations, not Hancom-calibrated constants.
- Signed character position offsets move the baseline in units of nominal font
  size; Hangul, Latin, Hanja, Japanese and other-script values are selected by
  character. Specialized symbol/user language classification remains limited.
- Explicit relSz and position values remain independent of script flags: size is
  multiplicative, shifts additive. Simultaneous super/sub flags prefer super.
- Underlines and strikeouts follow the adjusted glyph baseline and size. Slanted
  ink extents feed fitting, and shifted ascent/descent feed measured local flow.
- These apply to ordinary text runs (including table/textbox/note bodies); specialized
  ruby/overlay/auto-number controls still use their separate typography paths.

fixture (not Hancom reference output), accompanied by binary flag/sign tests.

## Long notes and oversized content (2026-10-14)

Long text footnotes now reserve a bounded first fragment and continue at complete
saved-line boundaries. Dedicated continuation pages are drained before the body
resumes. Configured columns, separators and page decorations are carried across;
the original first-line number is not duplicated. This is a content-preserving
policy, not Hancom's exact shared body/footnote pagination. A single indivisible
note line taller than a full page still errors explicitly; rich note objects and
complex multi-column continuation require further fixtures.

Root tables containing an individually page-tall nested object can be fitted
proportionally as a unit, retaining vector text, borders and nested content.
Ordinary multi-page nested tables keep their existing row/line splitting (the
three-page inner-table fixture is pinned by a regression assertion). Oversized
inline/paragraph-relative foreground objects without captions can likewise fit
the printable area. Background/paper-relative content is not generally resized.
Fitting is reported with its scale; this is NOT general structural nested-table
pagination. Very dense tables may become too small; oversized captions, complex
rotated envelopes and mixed nested-object/footnote cases remain limited.

## Shared body / continued footnotes (2026-10-14)

The previous dedicated-page policy is superseded for ordinary body flow. Unfinished
note rows are queued per column; subsequent body lines reserve a bounded note area.
At page finalization, unused body space can hold additional complete note lines.
New note rows follow the carried rows without repeating or dropping text. Only the
remaining tail at a section/document end is drained on note-only pages.

The 90-line example now uses 3 pages rather than 4, with body and continued notes
on page 2. Tests cover every line exactly once, shared-page content, and ordering
when a second note is introduced. 47 tests pass, Clippy passes. This remains a
bounded-reservation layout policy, not exact Hancom pagination. Column/section
changes with complex note objects and simultaneous endnotes need more coverage.
Next priority remains structural splitting of oversized nested tables, followed
by mixed overflow objects and general paragraph reflow. Outputs: `output/shared-footnotes/`.

## Nested text-table structural pagination (2026-10-14)

Oversized nested text tables can now paginate without shrinking glyphs or table
columns. A measured vector representation supplies cell rules/fills and actual
font ink extents. Safe row boundaries are preferred; otherwise complete text/ink
bands determine cuts. Only borders and backgrounds are fragmented. Vertical
merged cells continue across page edges without duplicating their text; no artificial
horizontal rule is inserted through a continued cell. Following body flow resumes
after the final table fragment.

Current eligible content: text, axis-aligned rules, solid rectangular fills and
basic straight decoration paths. Footnotes/endnotes/captions in the table, repeated
headers, rotations/transforms, images and other vector primitives keep the prior
reported fallback. General structural splitting of those combinations remains work.
This is not Hancom-calibrated pagination and relies on stored line geometry.

## Repeated headers and captions on nested-table fragments (2026-10-14)

from the table hierarchy and repeats it on subsequent active fragments at original
scale. Repeated header height is reserved before selecting the next body-row cut.
Eligible headers have a bounded first row without vertical spans crossing into
the body; unsupported requested headers keep the reported fitting fallback.

TOP/BOTTOM captions attached to the outer split table are handled once: top captions

## Mixed table pictures, transformed objects and footnotes (2026-10-14)

cropped images, Bezier/path shapes and balanced transformation groups retain their

## Plain-paragraph reflow (2026-10-14)

Default conversion preserves valid saved line caches. If a section has a top-level
paragraph without a cache, its ordinary text paragraphs are reflowed using the
selected font's glyph advances/ink extents. `--reflow-text`,
`convert_with_reflow(..., true)` and `Engine::render_reflow_text()` explicitly
recalculate cached plain-text sections as well. Source documents are not modified.

Implemented: UTF-16-safe line boundaries, word-oriented Latin wrapping with a
long-word fallback, CJK character/word modes and basic punctuation protection,
explicit/empty line breaks, mixed character styles, HWPUNIT margins/indent,
paragraph spacing and percent/fixed/minimum/between-line spacing. Page placement
supports keepLines, keepWithNext, pageBreakBefore, two-line widow/orphan protection
where space permits, and sequential fixed-column flow. HWP paragraph flags/margins
and both legacy/current line-spacing fields are normalized into the shared model.

Unsupported reflow content fails explicitly rather than disappearing: numbered/
bulleted paragraphs, inline/floating objects, equations, notes, RTL and mid-section
column changes. Table/textbox/header/footer reflow, tab-stop semantics (missing
width falls back to 24pt), full shaping/hyphenation/CJK line-breaking rules and
column balancing remain work. Fixed spacing is enlarged when necessary to contain
glyph ink; exact Hancom layout equivalence is not claimed.

References: [HWP 5.0 rev 1.3, paragraph shape tables 43–46](https://cdn.hancom.com/link/docs/한글문서파일형식_5.0_revision1.3.pdf)
and [Hancom OWPML break-setting model](https://github.com/hancom-io/hwpx-owpml-model/blob/main/OWPML/Class/Head/breakSetting.cpp).

Evidence: generated cacheless HWPX renders 7 pages, fixed two-column HWPX renders
5 pages, and forced reflow of the equivalent cached HWPX produces the identical
PDF bytes. Public `samples/web/paragraphs.hwp` also reflows successfully (1 page).
Text preservation, font-size-dependent line/page counts, five page heights for
heading/keep rules, UTF-16 offsets, hard breaks and unsupported-content rejection
are tested. 63 tests and all-target Clippy pass. Existing 43 documents/210 pages
remain pixel-identical in default mode, with unchanged source hashes/page/object
counts and zero geometry findings. New example QA also has zero findings.
Fixtures: `samples/reflow/`, generator: `examples/reflow_fixture.rs`.

## Mixed-content reflow (2026-10-14)

This increment extends the preceding plain-paragraph scope. The preferred CLI
flag is now `--reflow`; `--reflow-text` and `render_reflow_text()` remain aliases.
`Engine::render_reflow()` handles supported mixed sections. Default cached
conversion remains unchanged; automatic recovery is triggered by missing
 top-level paragraph caches, not necessarily missing nested-only caches.

Table cells recursively rewrap at their actual inner widths. Column widths can
shrink to the container, row heights grow while preserving declared minima, and
merged-cell height requirements propagate to rows. Existing table splitting,
repeated headers, pictures, rotated rectangles and footnote continuation are
reused. Body text resumes after the final table fragment and caption. Inline
pictures retain inline placement; floating supported objects become blocks in
document order. Footnotes rewrap at the note-area width and their space is reserved
when selecting body lines. Plain top/bottom captions reflow too.

Explicit limits: table reflow requires single-column body flow; text wrapping
around floating objects, background objects, side/note-bearing captions, general
shape/group reflow, numbered/bulleted paragraphs, equations, endnotes, RTL and
mid-section column changes remain unsupported. Header/footer caches are not
generally rebuilt. Exact Hancom pagination and full shaping remain unverified.

## Paragraph-leading float wrapping (2026-10-15)

Reflow supports one leading SQUARE picture/rectangle in a top-level unnumbered
paragraph, aligned LEFT/RIGHT relative to PARA/COLUMN, vertically TOP relative
to PARA, with zero source position offsets and no caption. Outer margins and
rotated bounding boxes define a rectangular exclusion, with a 1pt ink clearance.
Line widths shrink beside the object and restore below its bottom. The initial
object-height text band stays on one page/column, including footnote reservation;
subsequent text can continue normally. A short paragraph reserves trailing height
so the next paragraph clears the object. Widow adjustment cannot split the initial
float band. Objects leaving less than 24pt of usable side text fall back to block
placement. An object/text band taller than a usable column fails explicitly.

Scope limits: cross-paragraph floats, multiple simultaneous floats, centered or
contour wrapping, nonzero anchor offsets, captioned floats, numbered paragraphs
and floats in cells/text boxes retain the prior block fallback. This does not
claim all Hancom SQUARE/text-flow variants or exact original line breaking.
Saved-layout conversion is unchanged.

Generated single-column (3 pages) and two-column (2 pages) examples cover both
sides, rotated rectangle text, short body text and an anchored footnote. Tests vary
page height, check source-character preservation, line-width restoration, object
and reference placement, geometry QA and a full-width-picture fallback. Generator:
examples/float_reflow_fixture.rs; tests: tests/reflow_floats.rs. Local results:
output/reflow-floats/SUMMARY.html. These are controlled generated fixtures, not
Hancom-reference exports.

All 75 Rust tests and all-target Clippy pass. All 43 prior documents / 210 pages

## Cross-paragraph float continuation (2026-10-15)

A supported leading float now carries its remaining rectangular exclusion into
following ordinary body paragraphs. Each paragraph retains its margins, indentation
and before/after spacing. The line breaker can leave unused float height for the

## General float positions and opt-in contours (2026-10-15)

The general reflow path supports pictures, rectangles, ellipses and polygons at
paragraph-relative vertical offsets and paragraph/column-relative horizontal
LEFT/CENTER/RIGHT offsets. Up to 32 objects per paragraph are accepted; anchors
can occur after text or in a later paragraph while older regions remain active.
Connected float/text/note bands move together. If overlap is disabled, intersecting
object bounding boxes are separated vertically with a report warning. Horizontal
positions outside a printable column are clamped with a warning; negative vertical
anchors fail explicitly. PAGE/PAPER absolute anchors remain saved-layout territory.

Source SQUARE semantics remain rectangular by default. The explicit
--contour-wrap / Engine::render_contours() /
convert_with_options(input, output, fonts, force, contours) option enables
geometric contour approximation and forces reflow. Ellipses use 128 segments;
polygons use source vertices; rectangles use transformed corners. Every candidate
line band is conservatively projected through the regions with margins and stroke
clearance. The largest free interval is selected; a fully blocked band advances
below obstacles. LEFT_ONLY/RIGHT_ONLY restrict selection. BOTH_SIDES currently
uses one largest interval, not multiple fragments on the same baseline.

Official references: Hancom's
[shape serialization](https://github.com/hancom-io/hwpx-owpml-model/blob/main/OWPML/Class/Para/AbstractShapeObjectType.cpp)
and [enum definitions](https://github.com/hancom-io/hwpx-owpml-model/blob/main/OWPML/Class/enumdef.h).
The checked model exposes SQUARE/TOP_AND_BOTTOM/BEHIND_TEXT/IN_FRONT_OF_TEXT and
four textFlow directions. Thus geometric contours are an engine option, not an
invented source-format enum or an assertion of Hancom-identical contour semantics.
Binary HWP textFlow decoding is unchanged; direction selection uses available
normalized metadata.

Limits: images retain rectangular exclusion (no alpha contour); Bezier/arc/group
contours, internal holes/THROUGH behavior, captioned and cell/textbox floats,
negative vertical and page/paper anchors remain work. A connected float region
larger than one usable column fails rather than clipping content. Existing list,
equation/endnote and complex section reflow limits still apply. Geometry QA checks
are supplemented by polygon-band tests and page images; they do not establish

## Roadmap stage 2 — anchors and oversized objects (2026-10-15)

Negative paragraph-relative offsets reserve lead-in space so the anchor paragraph
moves down without overlapping the previous paragraph. PAGE/PAPER positions are
normalized on a new reflow page; source coordinates are retained within the paper.
Both SQUARE and absolute TOP_AND_BOTTOM are supported. Later columns conservatively
clear a page-wide floor beneath absolute objects. This may use more whitespace
than Hancom; absolute anchors outside the paper remain explicit errors.

Oversized floats/groups fit the usable area with a reported uniform scale. Already
fitted groups suppress a second fit of their members, preserving relative sizes.
A long chain of later anchors clears the previous region before continuing; an
oversized same-paragraph connected set uses sequential blocks with a warning.
Splittable tables still consume remaining column space and retain their previous
pagination. Internal mixed headers and exact absolute-anchor pagination remain
subjects for the final reference comparison and later stage integration.

Six examples (11 pages) cover negative offsets, PAGE/PAPER coordinates, absolute
objects in two columns, a large image, a large group, and a long chain. Additional
tests cover absolute TOP_AND_BOTTOM, square/contour equivalence for image anchors,
member scale ratios and sequential fallback. All 100 tests and all-target Clippy
pass. Baseline 43 documents / 210 pages retain source hashes, page/object counts,
zero geometry findings and pixel-identical output. The two stage-1 PDFs remain
byte-identical. New code resides primarily in src/layout/anchors.rs and the
